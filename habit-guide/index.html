The habit guide

Six ideas that make a habit stick, with the research behind them, and a 30-day tracker you can print. Free; nothing to sign up for.

Six ideas

  1. Start smaller than feels worth it

    Pick a version of the habit you could do on your worst day: one push-up, one page, two minutes. On good days do more if you like, but the small version is the one that counts. You are building the habit of showing up first.

  2. Tie it to something you already do

    Write it as a plan: After I [something I already do], I will [the habit]. For example: after I pour my morning tea, I will read one page.

    Plans in this “when… then…” form are called implementation intentions. A review of 94 studies with more than 8,000 people found they help people do what they meant to do, by a medium-to-large amount.

  3. Expect about two months, not 21 days

    In a study that followed people building a new daily habit, it took 66 days on average for the habit to feel automatic, and anywhere from 18 to 254 days depending on the person and the habit. If it still feels like effort after three weeks, nothing is wrong.

  4. Missing one day does not undo it

    The same study found that a single missed day had little effect on how the habit formed. What hurts is the second day, when a slip turns into a new routine. So the rule is simple: never miss twice.

  5. Make it easy to start and hard to skip

    Put the book on your pillow, the shoes by the door, the water bottle on your desk. Make the thing you want to stop doing a little harder: the phone in another room, the snacks on a high shelf. Fewer steps to start means fewer chances to talk yourself out of it.

  6. Tick it off where you will see it

    Mark the day right after you do the habit, not at night from memory. A row of ticks is a small reward in itself, and it shows you honestly how the month is going. The sheet below is made for that.
